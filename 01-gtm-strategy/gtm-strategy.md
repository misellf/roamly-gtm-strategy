## 1. Discover Framework

### Align on the Goal
- **Core business problem:** Roamly supports individual and small-party bookings well, but larger groups must coordinate across separate accounts, manage payments manually, and align availability and preferences outside the product. These workarounds create friction, booking drop-off, and additional support needs.
- **What success looks like:** Groups can choose an experience, coordinate plans, and pay in a way that works for their members. More group booking attempts become completed bookings, support needs decline, and the individual booking experience remains smooth. Roamly strengthens its reputation as a place to plan authentic local experiences together and gives group members a reason to return.

### Do the Research
- **Signals of a real opportunity:** Customers are already attempting larger group bookings across separate accounts. Support has noticed a growing pattern of requests, customers are manually splitting payments and syncing availability, and drop-off is high. Feedback consistently indicates that people want to book on Roamly but find the product gets in their way. Roamly also has a trusted brand, strong repeat booking, and a host network of more than 12,000 vetted experts.
- **Top 5 possible segments:** Friends traveling together; families and multigenerational groups; celebration groups, such as birthdays and reunions; corporate teams and offsites; clubs and community groups.
- **How to size the opportunity:** Analyze group booking attempts, party sizes, searches, abandoned sessions, support tickets, and customer feedback by city and experience type. Compare conversion, booking value, repeat behavior, cancellations, and payment patterns across party sizes. Interview organizers and group members to learn who initiates, decides, and pays. Ask Host Operations about capacity and availability, and work with Finance to assess booking frequency, payment costs, support costs, and host economics.

### Build a Working Team
- **Cross-functional stakeholders:** Product, Design, Engineering, Data, Customer Support, Host Operations, Marketing and Brand, Finance and Payments, and Trust, Safety, and Legal. Include Sales or Partnerships if research supports a corporate group offering.

### Form Your Hypothesis
- **Most valuable segment to target first, and why:** Friends traveling together, especially groups already browsing Roamly. The scenario shows people trying to coordinate bookings, align preferences and availability, and split payment. Roamly can reach them through its existing discovery and booking journeys, making this segment practical to test. Validate the choice against families and celebration groups during research and the pilot.
- **Core value proposition:** Friends traveling together can choose and book a Roamly experience as one group, coordinate the plan, and split payment without managing separate bookings and workarounds themselves.

## 2. GTM motion
- **Chosen motion:** PLG
- **Why:** The product is self-serve, with a short path from discovering an experience to starting a group booking. Organizers can invite friends through the product, creating a natural way to bring new travelers to Roamly. Invitations support product-led growth; the motion does not depend on a broader community or a sales team.

## 3. Primary goal
Acquire new customers through completed group bookings. Existing customers may also use Roamly Groups, but the primary measure is new travelers who join and complete a group booking.

## 4. The assumption I'm pushing back on
Invited friends will join and pay through Roamly instead of moving coordination to a group chat or using another payment method. Test this in a limited launch by measuring invite acceptance, member participation, payment completion, booking conversion, new-customer bookings, and support contacts. Interview organizers and invitees who abandon the flow to understand why.

## 5. V1 GTM strategy one-pager
**Audience:** Friends traveling together, especially groups already showing intent to book a Roamly experience.

**Promise:** Make it easy to plan and book an authentic local experience as a group.

**Motion:** PLG, with product invitations as the primary acquisition loop.

**Primary goal:** Acquire new customers through completed group bookings.

**Initial proof points:** Group booking conversion, invite acceptance, payment completion, new-customer bookings, and support contacts.

**Guardrail:** Keep individual and small-party booking smooth.

**Main risk to test:** Invitees may not complete coordination or payment in Roamly.
