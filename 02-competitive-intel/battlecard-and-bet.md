## 1. Primary competitor
- **Competitor:** Airbnb Experiences
- **Core offering:** Local-hosted tours, classes, workshops, and other activities. Private group bookings are available when the host offers them, with a shared message thread after booking.
- **How they position themselves:** A way for travelers to connect with local experts and experience a destination through activities hosted by people who know it.
- **Primary audience:** Travelers and local groups looking for activities to do together, including families, work outings, and social gatherings.
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Pricing varies by experience and host. A private group may pay the host’s minimum price if it exceeds the total price for the group. Airbnb says a reservation paid in full cannot be split across multiple payment methods.
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** Airbnb already offers private group bookings, group messaging, strong brand recognition, and broad marketplace selection. It may be good enough for groups whose organizer is willing to choose, pay, and collect from everyone afterward.

## 2. Battlecard

### Know the Landscape
- **Overview:** Airbnb Experiences is a direct competitor for travelers seeking local activities to enjoy together.
- **Key offerings:** Local-hosted activities, private group reservations when available, guest invitations, and group messaging after booking.
- **Pricing:** Varies by experience and host. Private group minimum pricing may apply; paid-in-full reservations cannot be split across multiple payment methods.

### Understand the Battlefield
- **Key strengths:** Brand and reach; broad discovery and selection; private group booking; post-booking communication with the host.
- **Key weaknesses:** Private group availability depends on the host; payment cannot be split across multiple methods at checkout; groups still need to agree on an experience before booking.

### Win the Deal
- **Why Roamly wins:** Roamly can bring shared planning, booking, and split payment into one group flow, supported by curated experiences and a network of 12,000+ vetted hosts across 40 cities.
- **Why Airbnb wins:** Airbnb may be preferable when travelers prioritize brand familiarity, marketplace breadth, or booking activities alongside their Airbnb stay. It can also work when one person is comfortable paying upfront and settling up later.
- **Quick dismiss:** “Airbnb is a strong place to discover experiences and book private groups. Roamly Groups is built to help the whole group plan and pay together.”
- **Landmine questions:**
  - Does one person usually pay the full amount and collect from everyone afterward?
  - Do you need everyone’s input before the booking is confirmed?
  - How do you agree on the date, experience, and itinerary today?
  - What happens when someone can’t attend or the group size changes?
- **Objection handling:**
  - **“Airbnb already has local experiences.”** True. Roamly is designed for groups that need to coordinate the choice and payment together.
  - **“We can book Airbnb and split the cost ourselves.”** That can work if one person is willing to pay upfront and manage reimbursements. Roamly is for groups where that work gets in the way of booking.
  - **“Airbnb has group messaging.”** It does, and that helps after booking. Roamly’s opportunity is to support the group from planning through booking and payment.
  - **“Airbnb has more options.”** It may, depending on the destination. If breadth is the priority, Airbnb may be the better fit. Roamly should compete on curated choice and a smoother group booking process.

## 3. What I want to revisit in my Module 1 strategy

I want to revisit my value proposition because Airbnb already supports private group bookings and messaging. Roamly needs to stand out by making it easier for friends to agree on the experience and split payment as part of one shared booking—not just by letting them book together.

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** Friends traveling together, especially groups already browsing Roamly. The scenario shows real group-booking demand, but the friend segment remains a hypothesis to validate in the pilot.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** The whole group can align on an experience, plan together, and split payment in the same booking flow.
- **The biggest threat to that position:** Airbnb’s brand, broad selection, private group bookings, and group messaging. It may be good enough if one friend is willing to organize, pay, and settle up afterward.

### Insight
Airbnb already supports private group bookings and group messaging, so “book as a group” is not distinctive by itself. Roamly needs to make the group decision and payment easier before the booking is confirmed.

### Belief
I now believe Roamly Groups wins when friends can make and complete a booking together, because it removes the planning and payment workarounds that still fall on the group organizer.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** Focusing first on friends already browsing Roamly and making shared planning, booking, and split payment one simple, self-serve flow.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** Families, celebration groups, and corporate teams as launch segments, along with trying to match Airbnb’s full marketplace breadth. I’ll validate the friend-group use case first, then use pilot results to decide where to expand.
