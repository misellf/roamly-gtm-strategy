# Messaging Pillars · Roamly Groups

## 1. From/To market shift

- **From, life before:** Friends planning an activity have to coordinate across searches, chats, and separate accounts. The organizer gathers preferences, checks schedules, makes the booking, and often pays upfront. That work can lead the group to compromise or abandon the booking.
- **To, life after:** Friends can agree on a plan, see it in one shared itinerary, and commit to a group booking with each person paying their share. The organizer no longer has to carry the planning and payment burden alone.

## 2. Audiences

- **Number of audiences that need their own message:** 2
- **Why you picked this number:** The launch still focuses on one segment—friends traveling together—but there are two distinct roles in the booking. The organizer initiates the plan and carries the coordination burden; invited friends need to understand the plan and join and pay easily. Each role needs a message that speaks to its own job.

## 3. Messaging pillars

### Audience 1: The friend who starts the Roamly search and takes responsibility for getting the group to a decision and booking.
- **Value prop:** Turn interest in an experience into a confirmed group plan without becoming everyone’s travel coordinator or paying the full cost upfront.
- **Capabilities:** Start a group booking and invite friends.
Share an itinerary the group can use to coordinate.
Let members pay their share as part of the group booking.
- **Evidence:** Roamly’s existing platform has high category ratings, repeat booking at three times the industry average, and a network of 12,000+ vetted hosts across 40 cities. These support trust and experience availability, but they do not yet prove the new group flow’s impact. The pilot should measure group booking completion, invite acceptance, payment completion, and support contacts. “8 in 10 group bookings were paid for by more than one traveler.”
“85% of organizers said the shared itinerary made it easier to get the group aligned.”

### Audience 2: A friend invited to join a group booking who wants to understand the plan and participate without creating extra coordination.
- **Value prop:** Know what the group has planned and join without relying on one friend to manage the details or pay everyone’s share.
- **Capabilities:** View the shared itinerary.
Join the group’s booking.
Pay their share through the group booking flow.
- **Evidence:** Roamly’s strong ratings, repeat booking, and vetted host network can reassure invitees about the experience they’re joining. Group-specific proof is still needed; the pilot should track invitee joins, individual payment completion, and feedback on the shared plan. “3 in 4 invited friends paid their share through the group booking.”
“85% of participants said they could clearly see the group’s plan before paying.”

## 4. Copy across three surfaces

- **Social post:** “Planning a Roamly experience with friends? Start one group booking, share the itinerary, and let everyone pay their share—without becoming the group’s coordinator and covering the bill upfront.”
- **Product tour opening line:** “Start your group booking, invite your friends, and keep the itinerary and everyone’s share of the cost together.”
- **In-app notification:** “Your group plan is ready to share. Invite your friends so everyone can see the itinerary and pay their share.”
